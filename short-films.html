TITLE / CONCEPT NOTE

QCO

2023–2026

Through QCO, I wanted to convey that imagination, technology, and human emotions can shape a better future—but only if humanity stays connected to nature, creativity, and compassion.

In the story Titas, a boy living in the changing landscape of the suburbs. With his pet dog, Gulabo. Their bond is tested when his father brings home QCO, an advanced AGI robot that begins to dominate Titas’s attention. A late-night confrontation through futuristic visions and time-travel-like experiences, the story shows a future Earth damaged by environmental destruction, uncontrolled urbanization, and the disappearance of animals and natural life, while humanity lives in clinical, floating sky-cities.

I also highlight how technology can both help and isolate people. QCO helps Titas learn, create, and innovate, but Gulabo, the pet companion, slowly becomes emotionally neglected. This contrast suggests that while artificial intelligence and advanced technology are powerful tools, human relationships and emotional bonds must not be forgotten.

Upon returning to the present, Titas is no longer just a dreamer, he collaborates with his father to build the "FlyBycycle" which becomes a symbol of his indomitable willpower. Titas transforms his dream into reality through learning, research, and creativity, showing that innovation begins with curiosity and courage.

Finally, the story warns about climate change and reckless modernization. The frightening vision of flooded, broken cities reflects the possible future consequences of humanity’s current actions. At the same time, the ending leaves hope—that future generations can still rebuild a better world through knowledge, creativity, and empathy.

TITLE / CONCEPT NOTE

MASK & SANITIZER

2020

When I realized I could use my skills and knowledge to tell stories and bring my surreal imagination to life, I began experimenting with animation. Without any formal knowledge of animation, I used only my phone and free apps like Autodesk SketchBook and KineMaster. After three months of learning and experimenting, I created my first 10-minute animated short film. Although I later felt it could be improved and decided to keep it private, it remains an important milestone in my journey.
